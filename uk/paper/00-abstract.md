# Abstract

Evaluating large language models for clinical applications requires assessment beyond automated metrics, yet most benchmarks rely solely on token-level measures such as F1. We present FHIRBench-UK, an open benchmark evaluating five LLMs across six serialisation formats and three clinical tasks on 100 UK Core FHIR patient bundles, generating 9,000 evaluations scored on both token-level F1 and four-dimension clinical quality via cross-model judging. We observe a complete ranking inversion between layers: Llama 3.3 ranks first on F1 but last on clinical quality, whilst Claude Sonnet 4.5 ranks first on quality but third on F1—replicating findings from US FHIR data and confirming cross-national generalisability. Clinical template serialisation achieves 94% token reduction with minimal quality impact, enabling cost-effective NHS deployment. We propose a three-tier practitioner decision framework integrating safety requirements, cost constraints, and throughput demands. Multi-layer evaluation is essential for responsible clinical AI assessment; single-metric benchmarks produce misleading conclusions.
